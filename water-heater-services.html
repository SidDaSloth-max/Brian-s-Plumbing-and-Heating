Water Heater Repair, Replacement & Installation

A water heater failing on a Friday night is one of the most common emergency calls I take in Libby, MT — and it's exactly how one of my regular customers first found me, after a tank leak that couldn't wait until Monday. Water heaters here work harder than in a lot of places, because groundwater coming into the house is already cold, and the unit has to work that much harder to bring it up to temperature. That extra strain shows up as shorter tank life, more sediment buildup, and pilot lights or elements that fail sooner than the manufacturer's rated lifespan would suggest.

When a water heater starts failing, most homeowners notice one of a few things: lukewarm water that never gets fully hot, a popping or rumbling sound from sediment buildup at the bottom of the tank, rust-colored water, or an actual leak around the base of the unit. Any of those is worth a call before it turns into standing water on your floor.

Choosing the Right Water Heater for a Libby Home

Tank water heaters remain the most common option for homes in Libby and Troy, largely because they're straightforward to install and service, and they hold up well as long as they're sized correctly for the household. Tankless units are a good fit for smaller homes or cabins where space is tight or where a homeowner wants to stop paying to keep a full tank hot around the clock — but they need to be sized and installed correctly for Montana's cold incoming water temperatures, or performance suffers. I walk through both options honestly rather than pushing whichever one is more expensive, and I'll tell you plainly if a repair is a better call than a full replacement.

If you're dealing with a water heater that's lukewarm, leaking, or just old enough that you're wondering if today's the day it finally fails, call before it turns into an emergency. I service water heaters throughout Libby, Troy, Yaak, Trego, and the rest of Lincoln County.

How Long Should a Water Heater Last?

Most tank water heaters are built to last 8 to 12 years, though Libby's cold incoming water and, in some homes, harder well water can shorten that range if a unit isn't maintained. Tankless units, when installed and maintained correctly, often run 15 to 20 years — but that lifespan depends heavily on the installation being done right the first time, including proper venting and, in hard water areas, a scale-prevention system. If you know roughly when your current unit was installed, that's often the single best predictor of whether a repair still makes sense or whether you're better off planning for a replacement.

Routine maintenance extends that lifespan more than most homeowners realize. Flushing a tank water heater once a year clears out the sediment that settles at the bottom from Libby's water supply, which is one of the leading causes of premature tank failure and reduced efficiency. It's a service that takes me under an hour and typically pays for itself in lower energy bills alone.

What Happens During a Water Heater Replacement

When a tank water heater needs to be replaced, I remove the old unit, inspect the surrounding plumbing and gas or electrical connections for any needed updates to meet current code, install the new unit sized correctly for your household's hot water demand, and test it thoroughly before I leave. Most standard tank replacements are completed in a single visit. Tankless installations or conversions from tank to tankless take longer since they often involve new venting and gas line sizing, and I'll walk you through that timeline and cost before starting the work.

Frequently Asked Questions — Water Heaters

What size water heater do I need? Sizing depends on your household size and hot water usage patterns — I calculate this based on the number of bathrooms, appliances, and typical simultaneous use rather than just matching your old unit's size, since the old one may have been undersized to begin with.

Is it worth switching to tankless? It depends on your household and how the space is used. Tankless units cost more upfront but can lower ongoing energy costs and never run out of hot water — I'll give you an honest comparison for your specific situation rather than a generic recommendation.

My water heater is leaking — is that always a replace-it situation? Not always. A leak from a fitting or valve can often be repaired, while a leak from the tank itself usually means replacement. I can tell you which one it is during a quick inspection.
