Signs Your Heating System Needs Attention

A furnace that's cycling on and off more frequently than it used to, taking longer to bring a house up to temperature, making a new rattling or squealing noise, or driving up your heating bill without an obvious reason are all signs worth having checked before the coldest part of winter arrives. Catching a failing heat exchanger or a worn blower motor in October is a lot less stressful — and usually cheaper — than discovering it during a January cold snap.

I provide heating and furnace service throughout Libby, Troy, Yaak, Trego, and the rest of Lincoln County. If your heat has gone out or you want a system checked over before winter sets in, call anytime — I take emergency no-heat calls around the clock.

Why an Annual Tune-Up Matters in Lincoln County

A heating system that runs nonstop for months during a Montana winter accumulates wear a lot faster than one that only kicks on occasionally. An annual tune-up — checking the heat exchanger, cleaning or replacing filters, testing the ignition system, inspecting the blower motor, and verifying the thermostat is calibrated correctly — catches small problems while they're still cheap to fix. It also matters for safety: a cracked heat exchanger on a gas furnace can allow carbon monoxide into the home, and that's not something you want to discover the hard way. I recommend having this done in early fall, before the system is asked to run at full demand.

For homes with boilers rather than forced-air furnaces, the maintenance looks a little different — checking for mineral buildup in the system, testing the pressure relief valve, bleeding air from radiators or baseboard units, and inspecting the circulator pump — but the principle is the same. A boiler that's properly maintained runs more efficiently and lasts longer than one that only gets attention when something breaks.

Gas vs. Electric vs. Propane Heating

Libby and the surrounding area have a mix of heating fuel types depending on what infrastructure is available at a given property. In-town homes are more likely to have access to natural gas, while rural properties around Troy, the Yaak, and Trego more often run on propane or, in some cases, wood-fired systems. Each fuel type has different maintenance needs and different failure points — a propane furnace's regulator and tank setup, for instance, isn't something a natural gas technician necessarily has experience with, but it's a routine part of my work given how much of Lincoln County runs on propane.

Frequently Asked Questions — Heating & Furnace Service

How often should I get my furnace serviced? Once a year, ideally in early fall before the heating season starts, is enough for most residential systems to catch problems early and keep the system running efficiently.

My furnace turns on but doesn't heat the house — what's wrong? This can be several things, from a clogged filter restricting airflow to a failing blower motor or a thermostat that isn't communicating correctly with the system. I can usually diagnose it on the first visit.

Do you install new furnaces, or just repair existing ones? Both. If a repair no longer makes financial sense on an aging unit, I can recommend and install a properly sized replacement.
