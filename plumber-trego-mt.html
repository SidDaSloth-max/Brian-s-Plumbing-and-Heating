Plumbing & Heating Service in Trego, MT

Trego sits along Montana Highway 37 — the Lake Koocanusa Scenic Byway — northeast of Libby, in the shadow of the Kootenai National Forest. It's a small, close-knit community best known locally for the Dirty Shame Saloon, and for the lake homes and vacation cabins that line the shores of Koocanusa and the nearby Fisher River drainage. A good share of the properties out here are seasonal — occupied hard in summer and left unattended through the winter — which brings its own set of plumbing challenges.

Seasonal cabins are exactly where I see the most costly plumbing failures if they're not handled correctly: a water system that isn't properly winterized before the owners leave for the season, then a hard freeze that splits a pipe no one discovers until spring thaw floods the place. I handle both the preventive side — draining and winterizing a system before you close up for the year — and the repair side when something's already gone wrong.

Lake Homes Bring Their Own Plumbing Challenges

Properties along Lake Koocanusa deal with a few things that in-town Libby homes don't. Water tables near the shoreline swing with the reservoir's level, which the Army Corps manages seasonally, and that fluctuation puts extra strain on sump pumps, foundation drainage, and any plumbing run below grade near the water. On top of that, a lot of the homes and cabins along the lake were built as vacation properties first, which sometimes means supply lines and water heaters were installed to a lower standard than you'd want for year-round or even reliable seasonal use. When I get called out to a lake property for the first time, it's common to find at least one corner that was never quite finished to code.

The Fisher River side of the area, closer to the highway and away from the lakeshore, tends to have more traditional rural homes on well and septic systems, similar to what I see around Troy and the Yaak. Those come with the usual pressure tank and pump troubleshooting, plus supply lines that need protection from the same hard freezes that affect the rest of Lincoln County.

Getting a Cabin Ready for Winter — and Ready for Summer

The single most common job I do around Trego is seasonal turnover: draining and winterizing a system in the fall before the owners head out, then coming back in spring to reopen it, check for any freeze damage, and get the water running again before the first summer weekend. Doing this properly means more than just shutting off the main valve — it means draining traps, blowing out lines with air where needed, and protecting the water heater and any exposed fittings. Skipping steps is exactly how a cabin that looked fine in October turns into a flooded floor by May.
