A lot of the homes I work on in Libby, Troy, and the surrounding towns still have their original galvanized supply lines from decades ago. Galvanized pipe corrodes from the inside out, which means low water pressure and discolored water are often the first sign — long before a pipe actually bursts. If your water pressure has dropped over the years or you're seeing rust-colored water when you first turn on a tap, that's usually galvanized pipe on its way out, and it's worth having it looked at before it fails on its own schedule instead of yours.

I also see a fair amount of work tied to well systems on rural properties outside town — pressure tank issues, foot valves, and supply lines that freeze in exposed crawlspaces during a hard winter. If you're searching for a "plumber near me" or "pipe repair near me" anywhere in Lincoln County, I'm happy to take a look and give you a straight answer on what it'll take to fix it.

Signs You Need a Plumbing Repair Now, Not Later

A lot of plumbing problems give you warning before they turn into an emergency, if you know what to look for. A faucet or shutoff valve that's suddenly harder to turn is often an early sign of internal corrosion. A toilet that runs intermittently, even for a few seconds every so often, is wasting water and putting extra wear on the fill valve — and it's usually a five-minute fix. A slow drain that you've been living with for months isn't going to clear itself, and it's often a sign of a partial clog building toward a full blockage at the worst possible time. Catching these early almost always costs less than waiting for a full failure.

Water stains on a ceiling or the underside of a cabinet, even small ones, mean water is going somewhere it shouldn't, and by the time you can see it, it's often been happening for a while. The same goes for a water bill that jumped without an obvious explanation — that's frequently a sign of a hidden leak somewhere in the system, whether that's a running toilet, a slab leak, or a supply line leaking behind a wall.

What to Expect During a Repiping or Fixture Installation Project

A full or partial repipe is one of the bigger jobs I take on, and it's worth understanding what's actually involved before you commit to one. For a galvanized-to-PEX or galvanized-to-copper conversion, the water typically needs to be shut off for the duration of the work, so I plan the job to minimize how long you're without water — usually a single day for a typical single-family home, longer for larger or more complex layouts. I'll walk the house with you beforehand, identify where access points will be needed, and give you a clear estimate before any walls get opened up.

Fixture installation — new faucets, toilets, sinks, or a full bathroom remodel's plumbing — is usually more straightforward and can often be done in a single visit. Whether you've already purchased fixtures or want recommendations on brands that hold up well to Libby's water and cold winters, I can work with what you have or point you toward options that will last.
